Short bio

Dr. Alexander Abuabara is an Instructional Assistant Professor of Data Engineering and Industrial and Systems Engineering at Texas A&M University. He earned his B.Sc. in Civil Engineering from the University of São Paulo, his M.Sc. in Production Engineering from the Federal University of São Carlos, and his Ph.D. in Urban and Regional Science from Texas A&M University, where his research focused on probabilistic modeling and decision analysis.

An educator and scholar, Dr. Abuabara applies systems thinking and Bayesian probability to strengthen analytical judgment and risk analysis. He teaches courses in data engineering, decision and risk analysis, GIS, and engineering design, emphasizing computational reproducibility, interpretability, and practical problem-solving. His teaching prepares students to build robust data workflows, reason under uncertainty, and develop models that support informed and ethical decisions in complex systems.

Dr. Abuabara is active in academic service, having served as Faculty Senator for the College of Architecture and currently serving on the Undergraduate Affairs Committee in the Department of Industrial and Systems Engineering. His research spans logistics optimization, probabilistic modeling, risk assessment and management, and policy evaluation.

He is also interested in how coincidences, irrational beliefs, and cognitive biases shape human judgment, as part of a broader exploration of the psychological dimensions of risk and resilience. Through interdisciplinary collaboration and a commitment to open, transparent analytics, he contributes to advancing data engineering in strategic development and systems design.

Teaching statement

Teaching has always been one of the most energizing parts of my work, whether training colleagues, mentoring students, or leading my own classes. I teach and create educational content to help students better understand statistics, probability, and how data is collected, cleaned, and used to make decisions.

My teaching philosophy is grounded in a sincere desire to be an outstanding educator who adapts, learns, and improves. A classroom should be a place where students feel safe, supported, and inspired to succeed. Every semester is different and each group of students is unique; what worked once might not work again. I really want to be a great teacher and that means always learning, adapting, and getting better each and every day.

In my teaching, I work to create a structured yet dynamic environment that empowers students through guided autonomy and collaborative learning. I prioritize hands-on activities, applied learning that connects students to real-world problems and industries through practical projects aiming to build both competence and confidence.

Teaching has never been a simple job; it takes expertise and patience, creativity and heart. For me, teaching extends beyond lectures; it includes mentoring, fostering peer learning, and creating opportunities for students to build compelling portfolios and professional identities. Teaching isn’t just about delivering content; it’s about being present, responsive, and always be committed to the growth of each student.
